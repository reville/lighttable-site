LightTable Highly performant digital darkroom with realistic film stock effects.

  • Rust core with GPU-driven rendering on Metal.

    • 23 film stocks with stock-specific spectral response
    • Physical grain scaled by stock and film format
    • Capture exposure, white balance, and development controls
    • Print paper, exposure, preflash, and Y/M filtration
    • Halation, diffusion, glare, scan softness, and sharpening
  • Easy migration from Lightroom, with XMP and legacy .lrtemplate preset compatibility.

What makes LightTable great?

Start with the simple answer. Open each level when you want to see more of what is happening under the surface.

Level 0 Nerdery
  • It’s free.
  • The workspace stays quiet. The photograph stays large while the library, film controls, and editor share one window.
  • You can try it without moving your photographs. LightTable catalogs originals in place and stores its edits separately. It reads a copy of a Lightroom Classic catalog, so the original catalog never changes.
  • Film is a complete image pipeline. Choose a stock and format, then control development, print paper, filtration, preflash, grain, halation, and scan. A preset cannot model those stages.
Level 1 Nerdery
  • LightTable is open source. You can inspect how the renderer works and change the app yourself.
  • Spektrafilm ↗ carries the photograph from exposure through scan. It uses published sensitivity and density data to track dye formation, negative development, paper exposure, and the final scan.
  • Apple Vision stays on your Mac. If you turn it on, it adds searchable scene and object tags, visible text, face counts, and subject masks. It does not identify people or upload the analysis.
  • A Lightroom catalog import leaves Lightroom alone. LightTable copies the catalog and reads the copy. Ratings, flags, labels, keywords, collections, stacks, virtual copies, metadata, and supported develop settings come across.
  • No silent approximation. LightTable lists the controls it mapped and the controls it skipped when it imports Lightroom, Camera Raw, or Capture One settings. Different render engines produce different results, so the report labels converted settings approximate.
Level 2 Nerdery
  • A warmed 1100 px film render takes about 50 ms on the benchmark Mac. The measured median was 50.5 ms across 30 runs, using the resident Rust/WGPU renderer on an Apple silicon Mac running macOS 26.6.2.
  • Point Color, RGB curves, masks, and merge tools are built in. The same non-destructive edit can include four-way Color Grading, heal and clone, HDR, panorama or focus merge, soft proofing, versions, virtual copies, and command-line control.
  • Each film stock has its own model. Twenty-three stocks carry their own spectral sensitivity, dye absorption, density curves, masking, inhibitor behavior, halation, development, and grain. Film format changes the grain scale in the finished image.
  • The print and scan stages remain editable. Choose the print material, then adjust print exposure, preflash, yellow and magenta filtration, glare, scan softness, and sharpening. Reversal film goes straight to scan.
Level 3 Photo nerdy
  • Automatic lens correction requires a confident profile match. When Lensfun has the data, LightTable corrects calibrated distortion, transverse chromatic aberration, and lens shading. Otherwise it leaves automatic correction off and keeps manual controls available.
  • LibRaw 0.22 recognizes 1,275 cameras from 83 manufacturers. RAW enters the pipeline as scene-linear, 16-bit ProPhoto RGB. Capture white balance runs before film, with camera-native, fine-detail, and smooth-color demosaicing plus blend and reconstruct highlight recovery.
  • The pipeline stays floating point until export. JPEG, HEIF, and PNG are 8-bit; TIFF is true RGB16. Every export embeds the matching ICC profile for sRGB, Display P3, or ProPhoto RGB. Soft proofing covers sRGB, Display P3, matte paper, and gloss paper.
  • Preview and export use the same ordered grading math. The native Metal preview, WebGL fallback, and full-resolution exporter share the same curves and grading order. Saved edits and export sidecars record the recipe and renderer revision.
